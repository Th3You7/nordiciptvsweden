If you have just moved to Gävle, the question behind a search for IPTV Gävle is usually practical: what is already wired into the building, what am I paying for without knowing it, and what do I actually need to add? This guide answers that with local facts — the city network, the landlord, and the contracts that apply here.

Two things make this particular autumn worth watching in Gävle: the TV contracts in the city network change counterparty on 1 December 2026, and the copper network closes at the end of November. *Facts checked 12 November 2026.*

## How TV reaches a home in Gävle

Television arrives in Gävle by the same four routes as everywhere else in Sweden, but which one is cheapest depends on the building. The city network, cable TV in rental blocks, the terrestrial network through an aerial and streamed TV over the internet all exist here — and most households already pay for at least one.

Gävle is the seat of Gävle Municipality and the largest city in Gävleborg County, with [around 86,500 inhabitants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%A4vle) — big enough for full fibre coverage in town, small enough that the rural edges of the municipality still matter. The national picture behind these four routes is in [how digital TV works in Sweden](/en/blog/digital-tv-sverige); this article stays with what is local.

## GavleNet: the city network Gävle Energi owns

The city network here is called GavleNet and is part of Gävle Energi, the municipal utility. According to [the company's own page](https://gavlenet.se/) the network supplies broadband in Gävle and Ockelbo, and the customer chooses a speed between 100 and 1000 Mbps. Municipal ownership is more than trivia if you watch television.

It means the fibre and the TV service can be two separate deals with two separate counterparties. When the picture stutters, the network is what you troubleshoot; when a channel disappears, it is the service provider. That distinction is the whole difference between a call that goes somewhere and half an hour on hold.

| | Who you talk to | When |
|---|---|---|
| The fibre into the building | GavleNet / Gävle Energi | outages, low speed, new connection |
| The TV service | your TV provider | channels, account, invoice |
| The broadband service | your internet provider | the connection, the router |
| Cable TV in a rental block | the building's provider | basic line-up, fault reports |

## New on 1 December 2026: Sappa takes over the TV contracts

This is the concrete change this autumn for anyone in Gävle with TV through the city network. GavleNet states on its own page that from 1 December 2026 Sappa takes over the customer contracts for the TV service, and that the channel line-up, the prices and the way the service works are not affected. The counterparty changes, not the content.

In practice there are three things worth checking when the letter arrives:

- **Who the invoice comes from** after 1 December, so that a new sender is not mistaken for an attempted fraud.
- **Which terms carry across** to the new counterparty — in particular any fixed term and the notice period.
- **That the line-up looks the same** as before the handover, since that is precisely what is promised.

## Renting from Gavlegårdarna? You already pay for TV

Then you already have television, and that changes the whole calculation. The municipal housing company is Gävle's largest landlord, and according to [Gavlegårdarna's own information](https://www.gavlegardarna.se/hyr-bostad/flytta-in/) the basic line-up from Tele2, formerly ComHem, is included in the rent — while broadband is ordered separately through the communication operator Open Universe. Two contracts, two counterparties.

Three details in that arrangement are worth reading before you add anything:

- **The basic package is already paid for** through your rent. It does not disappear because you add a streamed service, and it is rarely possible to deduct.
- **Extra channels are ordered directly from Tele2**, not through the landlord — Tele2 is your counterparty for everything beyond the basic line-up.
- **Broadband is your own choice** through the Open Universe portal, so your speed is not fixed by the landlord.

For a tenant in Gävle the conclusion is therefore different from a homeowner's: start by reading the rent statement and the basic line-up, and add only what is genuinely missing. This matters most in your first months in Sweden, when it is easy to buy a service you were already provided with.

## The copper network closes at the end of November 2026

This change is national but lands on individual addresses, and November 2026 is the month. The Swedish Post and Telecom Authority states that the last parts of the copper network close at the end of November 2026. If your internet arrives through the telephone socket, it is the connection under your TV that is affected, not the TV service itself.

The authority's advice is to wait to be contacted by Telia, or by whichever operator is responsible for the service, and then to review the alternatives offered — [according to PTS](https://pts.se/internet-och-telefoni/teknikskifte/) the great majority can get telephony and internet through one of the options on the market. In Gävle town fibre is the usual replacement; further out in the municipality mobile broadband is the realistic one.

The order matters if this applies to you. There is no point signing up for a streamed TV service the week before your connection is replaced, because you would then be troubleshooting two things at once with no way to tell which is causing the stutter. Fix the connection first, confirm it is stable, and add TV after that — whichever provider you end up with.

## Which contract are you actually on?

Most people know what they pay in rent and what they pay for a phone, but not what the television costs. In Gävle the answer depends on how you live, and that question decides whether a streamed service is an addition or a replacement. The table below sets out the three common cases.

| You live in | You probably already have | What you are really choosing |
|---|---|---|
| A Gavlegårdarna rental | Tele2 basic line-up in the rent, broadband via Open Universe | an addition beyond the basic package |
| An owned flat or house on the city network | fibre from GavleNet, your own TV service | the whole TV route, freely |
| An older house on a telephone socket | copper, closing in November 2026 | a new connection first, TV second |

One concrete piece of advice before changing anything: write three numbers on the same sheet — what the rent includes in TV, what broadband costs per month, and what any extra channels cost at Tele2. Only with the three side by side can you see whether an addition is cheap or merely new.

## Regional news and local channels

This is the most common local question, and the answer is simpler than many expect. Regional news for the county is made by SVT Nyheter Gävleborg, and the programme *Lokala Nyheter Gävleborg* is broadcast on SVT and available afterwards on SVT Play. It is public service and costs nothing beyond what you already pay.

Two things are worth keeping apart here, because they are often confused:

- **The local coverage is already solved.** County news reaches you through the terrestrial network, through SVT Play in any app and through most TV packages — [according to SVT's own page](https://www.svt.se/nyheter/lokalt/gavleborg) the latest broadcast sits on SVT Play.
- **An IPTV service does not replace public service.** It adds something else: more channels, films and series. Anyone whose main interest is county news need not pay anybody for it.

That is also why we do not market ourselves against the names of local channels. What a service may distribute is a rights question, and anyone promising named Swedish channels without being able to show the agreement is promising something they cannot keep.
