An IPTV comparison looks simple: line the monthly prices up and pick the lowest. The trouble is that the headline number is rarely what you end up paying, and in autumn 2026 there are two concrete reasons for that.

The first is that Swedish operators have been cutting discounts rather than raising list prices. The second is that the sales channel itself can mislead — the consumer regulator issued an order against one provider this month. This guide shows what a comparison should contain and gives you the template to fill in. *Information checked 15 October 2026.*

## What an IPTV comparison should actually compare

Six facts decide what a subscription is worth. Price is one of them.

1. **Total cost across the whole period**, including what happens when the campaign ends.
2. **Simultaneous streams** — how many people can watch different things at once.
3. **Binding period and notice period.**
4. **Supported devices**, so you avoid buying hardware.
5. **Support**: channel, language and response time.
6. **What is genuinely included**, rather than what the marketing mentions.

The method for weighting these against each other is in [our guide to choosing a subscription](/en/blog/basta-iptv-abonnemang). This guide is about the numbers themselves and the traps inside them. What a reduced price must legally satisfy is covered separately in [what a price claim means](/en/blog/basta-och-billigaste-iptv).

Order matters. Start from what the household needs rather than from what is cheapest, or you will compare away requirements you actually have. A package with one stream can be perfect for one person and useless for a family, and that difference never shows in the price.

## The 2026 trap: campaign price versus list price

This is the most useful finding for anyone comparing Swedish TV prices this year.

A review of **27 comparable TV packages** at Telia, Tele2, Telenor, Boxer, Allente and Sappa checked prices on 24 February and 8 June 2026. The result: **15 packages had a higher campaign price**, one had become cheaper and 11 were unchanged. List prices largely stayed where they were, [as Dagens PS reported on 9 June 2026](https://www.dagensps.se/privatekonomi/tv-rabatterna-ryker-priset-hojs-med-101-procent/) (in Swedish).

| Package | Campaign price, February | Campaign price, June | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Allente Stream Basic | SEK 99/month | SEK 199/month | +101% |
| Boxer TV Bas | same increase | same increase | +101% |
| Tele2 TV Bas | same increase | same increase | +101% |
| Telia TV Mini | SEK 199/month | SEK 149/month | reduced |

*These prices applied on 8 June 2026 according to the review and may have changed since. Always confirm the current price with the provider before comparing.*

The point is not which operator raised the most. The point is **which number you are comparing**. A campaign price is temporary and has an end date; when it expires the list price takes over. Two packages that look equivalent during a campaign can differ by hundreds of kronor a month afterwards.

In practice: always write down both numbers, and calculate across the whole period you expect to keep the service.

## When the seller calls: October's regulator order

The second trap sits in how offers are presented, and it catches newcomers hardest — cold-call selling of TV and broadband is common in Sweden.

On 14 October 2026 the Swedish Consumer Agency published an **order against Allente for misleading telephone sales**, [listed in its own news feed](https://www.konsumentverket.se/aktuellt/) (in Swedish). Sellers had given the impression they were calling from the consumer's existing provider, and customers were promised cheaper subscriptions than they received. The order requires the company to state its name clearly and to say that the purpose of the call is to sell its subscriptions.

Three things follow for your comparison:

- **A phone call is not a comparison.** You cannot check the numbers while somebody is talking.
- **Ask for everything in writing.** A serious offer survives being emailed and read calmly.
- **Check who you are speaking to.** A seller mentioning your current provider does not mean the call comes from them.

The same principle applies whoever calls, including us: no serious seller demands an answer during the call.

### Five questions to ask before saying yes

Ask them in order and note the answers. Hesitation on any of them is itself an answer.

1. Which company are you calling from, and what is your full name?
2. What does the subscription cost after the campaign period, in kronor?
3. How long is the binding period, and what does breaking it cost?
4. Will you send the whole offer in writing before I decide?
5. What applies to the right of withdrawal, and how do I use it?

The fifth usually separates a genuine offer from a pressure tactic.

## What a comparison site will not show you

Search for an IPTV comparison and you meet dozens of ranking pages. Most earn from clicks or commission, which does not make them worthless — but it explains why the same handful of services top nearly every list.

Four quick checks decide whether a list is worth reading:

- **Is it dated?** Undated prices are not prices, they are memories.
- **Is the method explained?** What was compared, and how were the scores set?
- **Are any drawbacks mentioned at all?** A list with no objections is marketing.
- **Can you see who runs it?** No publisher means no accountability.

What no list can give you is how a service behaves on your network, on your TV, on a Friday evening. That part you test yourself, which is why a trial is worth more than a review.

One last thing: lists almost always compare campaign prices, because those look best. That is exactly the number that moved most during 2026.

## Working out the total cost

The method takes five minutes and reveals more than any ranking.

1. **Write down the campaign price** and how many months it runs.
2. **Write down the list price** that follows.
3. **Pick a horizon** — twelve months is a reasonable measure.
4. **Calculate:** (campaign price × campaign months) + (list price × remaining months).
5. **Divide by twelve** for the real monthly cost.

Using the numbers above: a package at 99 kronor for three months and 199 after lands at (99 × 3) + (199 × 9) = 2,088 kronor a year, or 174 a month — not 99.

Compare that with a flat 150 kronor and you get 1,800 kronor, cheaper across the year despite the higher headline.

### Three scenarios over twelve months

The same calculation on three common structures makes the difference concrete. These are examples, not offers.

| Structure | Months 1–3 | Months 4–12 | 12-month total | Average per month |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Campaign that doubles | SEK 99 | SEK 199 | SEK 2,088 | SEK 174 |
| Flat price, no campaign | SEK 150 | SEK 150 | SEK 1,800 | SEK 150 |
| Paid annually up front | — | — | SEK 1,560 | SEK 130 |

Three lessons. The lowest headline produced the highest annual cost. A flat price beat the campaign. And paying annually was cheapest per month but committed the most money to a service you may not yet know — which is the argument for testing first and committing second.

## The template: what to fill in

One row per option. The columns are what actually separates services.

| Field | What you write |
|---|---|
| Campaign price and length | e.g. SEK 99 for 3 months |
| List price | the price after the campaign |
| 12-month total | the calculation above |
| Binding period | none, 12 or 24 months |
| Notice period | how long, and how you cancel |
| Simultaneous streams | screens at the same time |
| Devices | does it run on your TV? (see [supported devices](/en/devices)) |
| Support | channel and response time |
| Right of withdrawal | what the terms say |

Three rows is enough. If a box is hard to fill for some option, that is itself an answer — information you cannot find before buying rarely gets easier to obtain afterwards.

Fill it in with offers actually in front of you, not list prices found elsewhere. The gap between a published price and what you are offered can be substantial, especially by phone or near the end of a campaign.

## Binding periods and cancellation

The law sets the limits. [According to Konsumentverket](https://www.konsumentverket.se/varor-och-tjanster/bindningstid-och-uppsagningstid/) (in Swedish), a consumer contract may have a binding period of at most 24 months, and for contracts from 1 May 2014 the notice period may be at most one month.

Two things deserve a specific check:

- **Does it renew automatically?** If so, how do you cancel and how far ahead?
- **What does breaking it cost?** The longer the commitment, the more an error costs.

In a market where campaigns change every few months, a short contract is worth more than a discount. The whole purchase process is in [buying IPTV safely](/en/blog/ip-tv-kopa).

## Compare sport separately

Sport is almost always the most expensive line on a TV bill, and the hardest to compare. Two rules make it manageable.

**Count per season, not per month.** A league runs a certain number of months. What matters is what you pay during them, plus any months you pay while nothing is played.

**Check what happens between seasons.** Can the subscription be paused, or does it roll on? Solutions differ most here, and price comparisons rarely say.

One note on line-ups: rights move between services, sometimes mid-contract. Don't build a long commitment on a particular sport staying where it is today. We never promise a named sport is included, and you should be sceptical of anyone who does.

## What a price comparison never shows

**Who you are contracting with.** Terms, contact routes and how the company behaves when something fails. The checklist is in [the company behind your IPTV](/en/blog/iptv-bolaget).

**What your connection can carry.** A cheap service on a weak home network is expensive in irritation. The test is in [our guide to internet TV](/en/blog/basta-internet-tv).

**How support actually answers.** Test before buying by asking a question and timing the reply.

**What is genuinely included.** Channel counts say less than whether your channels are there — and marketing counts whatever is countable. Ask instead for what can be checked: what is in the line-up the week you sign, how many screens work at once, and what happens when something breaks on a Sunday evening.

## Four mistakes in an IPTV comparison

1. **Comparing a campaign price against a list price.** The most common reason a comparison points the wrong way.
2. **Forgetting what the building already includes.** Many Swedish apartment blocks bundle a TV package into the monthly fee.
3. **Choosing by channel count.** 18,000 channels and 4,000 rarely differ in what a household watches in a week.
4. **Signing during a phone call.** See above.
